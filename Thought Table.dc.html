III.

The problem with fourteen

Keying the ending to a single dominant voice is the cheap version, and it shows: whichever voice you left loudest wins nearly every run. Fourteen endings, and most players will see four.

The version worth building keys to the top two voices, ordered. Actuary over Solidarity is a different person from Solidarity over Actuary — the first made peace with the price, the second is still angry about it. Twelve voices taken two at a time, order mattering, is 132 rows, and the pairs that never co-occur simply never get written.

That is the same coverage-grid problem as the statements, one layer up, and it is where the hundreds of combinations you described actually live. Four examples across the way, to see whether the shape holds.
