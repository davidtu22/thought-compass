I.

The five fields

Wants

The single thing it is always pushing for. This buys the agree / disagree split for free — knowing what a voice wants tells me what it says on any statement in the grid, in either direction.

Wrong about

The direction it is reliably mistaken in. The difference between a character and a mouthpiece. A voice that is simply correct is a narrator; a voice that is wrong the same way every time is someone you know.

Never

Words, moves, registers that are out of character. The drift guard — around line 150 every voice starts sounding like every other voice, and this is what catches it. Be fussy and specific.

Diction

Sentence length, vocabulary, whether it addresses you directly. The field I match against. Three lines in your own hand beat any description of it.

Fights

One voice it interrupts, one it defers to. This powers the cross-talk pairs, and it now also decides which ordered-pair Thought a run can land on.

how to work —

Take the eight drafts one at a time. Read the field, then either leave it or replace it. Leaving it is a real answer — it means I got the voice right.

the one to check —

Superstition. I wrote it as wrong in a way that keeps turning out useful, which may be too flattering. That is the voice where your read matters most.

filled for you —

You wrote “pick the other” on three of the defer slots. I picked; those are marked in the Fights field. Overwrite freely.
